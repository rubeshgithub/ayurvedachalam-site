# Shirodhara: Benefits and What to Expect

*By Maheswari Vedhachalam, BAMS (Bachelor of Ayurveda Medicine and Surgery) · Ayurveda Practitioner, Calgary · Reviewed [date set in CMS]*

Shirodhara is the Ayurvedic therapy in which warm oil is poured in a slow, steady stream onto the forehead for twenty to forty minutes. People book it for stress, poor sleep and a busy mind, and it is the therapy most new clients ask about by name. This article explains what it is, what clients commonly report, what the research can and cannot support, how a session runs here in Calgary, and who should wait or choose something else.

## What is Shirodhara?

The name comes from Sanskrit: shiro, head, and dhara, a stream. You lie on your back on a treatment table with a small bolster under your knees and your eyes covered. A copper or clay vessel hangs above your forehead with a narrow spout. Warm oil, usually a sesame-based herbal oil chosen for you, flows from the vessel in a thin, continuous stream that moves slowly from side to side across the forehead, above the eyebrows. The vessel is refilled so the stream never stops. The room is quiet and warm, and there is no conversation once the pour begins.

In Ayurvedic terms, Shirodhara is a calming therapy for the head and the nervous system, used when Vata, the quality of movement, is high: racing thoughts, light sleep, a feeling of being wound up. That is the tradition's language, and it is useful as a description of who tends to ask for it.

## What do people come to Shirodhara for?

In my practice, three things bring most people to this table.

- **A mind that will not slow down.** Clients commonly describe the first ten minutes as the sensation of the stream, and the rest as a state between awake and asleep. Many fall asleep. Most say the quiet lasts into the evening.
- **Sleep.** People with light, broken or late sleep often book a short series. Clients commonly report sleeping more deeply on the night of the session.
- **Tension held in the head, neck and jaw.** The warmth, the stillness and the forty minutes of not doing anything are, for many, the only time in the week that these muscles let go.

Those are reports, not results I can promise, and the research below is why I word it that way.

## What does the research say?

Less than the internet suggests, and it is worth being plain about it.

The best-known small trial is from India: thirty people with insomnia were divided into three groups of ten, one receiving a herbal tablet, one receiving Shirodhara with warm milk for fifteen days, one receiving both. All three groups improved on symptom scores and sleep diaries, with the combined group improving most. There was no placebo group, the sample was tiny, and the outcome measures were subjective, so the study shows that people felt better after the course, not that Shirodhara outperforms rest in a quiet room. A Japanese research group has published small physiological studies describing lowered measures of arousal during the pour in healthy volunteers, which fits what clients describe but again involves small numbers.

So: there is a consistent picture of people relaxing and sleeping better in small, uncontrolled studies, and no large controlled trial. I offer Shirodhara as a deeply restful therapy that many clients value for stress and sleep, not as a treatment for any diagnosed condition. If you have been told you have a sleep disorder, an anxiety disorder or depression, please keep your physician's care in place; Shirodhara can sit beside it, not instead of it.

## What happens in a 60-minute session in Calgary

1. **Arrival and a short check-in (5 minutes).** How you slept, anything new, any skin sensitivity. If it is your first visit, this follows a consultation, so I already know your history.
2. **Settling (5 minutes).** You lie down clothed from the waist down and covered; the head and neck are supported, hair is tied back and protected, eyes are covered with a soft pad.
3. **The pour (30 to 35 minutes).** The oil is warmed to just above body temperature and tested on your wrist. The stream begins at the centre of the forehead and moves slowly from temple to temple. You do not need to do anything.
4. **A short head and neck massage and rest (10 minutes).** The oil is worked gently through the scalp. You rest, then sit up slowly.
5. **After.** Your hair will be oily; we provide a towel and a cap, and most clients leave the oil in until the next morning. Plan a quiet evening, drink warm water, and avoid cold wind on your head, which in a Calgary winter means a toque.

The session is $130 for 60 minutes. Combined with a full-body Abhyanga it is 105 minutes for $230. Prices are in Canadian dollars before GST.

## How often should you have Shirodhara?

In the tradition, Shirodhara is given as a course: three, five or seven sessions on consecutive or alternate days. In a Canadian working life, most of my clients choose one of two patterns.

- **A short series of three** within two weeks when something has knocked their sleep or their nerves. Our package of three is $350.
- **A single session** every few weeks as maintenance, often paired with Abhyanga.

One session is a pleasant experience. The clients who describe lasting change in their sleep are almost always the ones who did a series and changed their evening routine at the same time, which is what we work on in the consultation.

## Who should avoid Shirodhara, or wait

Shirodhara is generally well tolerated. It is not offered, or is postponed, when:

- you are in the first trimester of pregnancy, or at any stage without your physician's agreement;
- you have a fever, a cold with heavy congestion, or a recent head injury;
- you have an open wound, rash or infection on the scalp or forehead;
- you have had a recent neck or spinal surgery;
- you have a known sesame or nut allergy (we can use a different base oil; tell us when booking);
- you have been told you have very low blood pressure and feel faint when lying still for long periods;
- you have a condition for which your physician has advised against lying flat.

If you have a diagnosed neurological or psychiatric condition, tell us before booking so we can plan around your care.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Shirodhara hurt or feel strange?**
It is warm oil, not pressure. The first minute can feel odd, like a slow drip, and then most people stop noticing the stream and drift.

**Can I drive home afterwards?**
Yes. You will feel relaxed rather than impaired, but give yourself ten minutes to sit up and have a glass of warm water first.

**How do I get the oil out of my hair?**
Apply shampoo to dry hair before wetting it, work it through, then rinse and wash a second time. Leaving the oil in overnight is traditional and good for a dry Calgary scalp.

**Is Shirodhara covered by insurance?**
On its own, no. It is not massage therapy, so it is not receipted as massage even though our practitioner is an RMT. If you book it combined with Abhyanga, only the massage portion is receipted.

**Is Shirodhara the same as an Indian head massage?**
No. Shiro Abhyanga (Indian head massage) is a hands-on oil massage of the head, neck and shoulders. Shirodhara is the poured stream, with only a short massage at the end.
