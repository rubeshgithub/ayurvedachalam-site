# Is Ayurveda Covered by Insurance in Canada?

*By Maheswari Vedhachalam, BAMS (Bachelor of Ayurveda Medicine and Surgery) · Ayurveda Practitioner, Calgary · Reviewed [date set in CMS]*

Usually not. Provincial health care does not cover Ayurveda anywhere in Canada, and most extended health plans do not list it either, because Ayurveda is not a regulated profession and insurers build their paramedical lists from regulated or association-recognised providers. There is one useful exception: massage therapy given by a Registered Massage Therapist is covered by most plans, and some Ayurvedic therapies are massage. This article explains what is and is not claimable at this practice, how the medical expense tax credit treats these services in Alberta, and the three questions to ask your insurer before you book.

## What provincial health care covers

Nothing here. Alberta Health Care covers physician and hospital services and a defined list of others. Ayurvedic consultations, Ayurvedic therapies, massage therapy and spiritual healing are all outside it, as they are in every other province.

## What extended health plans cover

Workplace and individual extended health plans include a "paramedical" section: a yearly maximum per practitioner type for services such as massage therapy, physiotherapy, chiropractic, acupuncture, naturopathy and psychology. Which types appear, and the dollar limit for each, is set by your employer's contract with the insurer, so two people in the same city can have very different coverage.

"Ayurveda" or "Ayurvedic practitioner" is not a category on the paramedical lists we have seen. Insurers draw those lists from professions that are regulated by a province or that have a recognised professional association, and Ayurveda has neither in Canada. So a consultation with me, Shirodhara, Kati Vasti and spiritual healing are, as a rule, paid out of pocket.

**Massage therapy is different.** Most plans cover massage therapy when it is performed by a Registered Massage Therapist who belongs to an association the insurer recognises. I am an RMT. When the service you receive is massage therapy, Abhyanga being the clearest example, you receive a massage-therapy receipt with my registration number, and you claim it as you would any massage.

## Which services at Ayurvedachalam can be receipted as massage?

We follow one rule: only what is massage therapy, performed by the RMT, is receipted as massage therapy.

| Shiro Abhyanga (head massage), Pada Abhyanga (foot massage) | Yes |

Receipts show the date, the service, the time, the amount and the RMT's name and registration number. We do not describe a non-massage service as massage on a receipt, and we would ask you not to request it; insurers audit, and a false receipt puts your coverage at risk as well as our registration. Where a plan requires a physician's note for massage, that is between you and your physician; the note is for massage therapy, which is what you will receive.

## The medical expense tax credit in Alberta

Canada's medical expense tax credit lets you claim eligible medical expenses on your tax return once they pass a threshold. For services, the person providing them must be an "authorized medical practitioner" in your province, and the Canada Revenue Agency publishes the list by province.

For Alberta, the CRA list includes naturopaths and acupuncturists, among others, but it does **not** list massage therapists, and it lists no Ayurvedic practitioner category. British Columbia and Ontario do list registered massage therapists. So for an Alberta resident, as the list stands today, neither Ayurvedic services nor massage therapy at this practice qualifies for the federal tax credit, even though massage may be covered by your benefits plan. If you file in another province, check the list for that province.

Health spending accounts (HSAs) are different again: they follow the CRA's eligible-expense rules, so the same Alberta limits apply, while wellness spending accounts set their own, usually broader, rules. Check with your plan administrator.

## Three questions to ask your insurer before you book

1. **"Does my plan cover massage therapy, and what is my yearly maximum?"** Also ask whether a physician's referral is needed and whether there is a per-visit cap.
2. **"Do you accept receipts from an RMT registered with [association name]?"** We will publish the association on our pricing page; have it to hand when you call.
3. **"Is there any coverage for Ayurvedic consultation or for services by an unregulated practitioner?"** Expect no, but a few wellness accounts say yes, and it is worth the one question.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I claim Shirodhara on my benefits?**
Not on its own. It is not massage therapy, so it is not receipted as massage. If it is booked combined with Abhyanga, the Abhyanga portion is receipted.

**Is an online Ayurvedic consultation covered by anything?**
Not by provincial health care or standard paramedical coverage. Some wellness spending accounts reimburse it; ask your administrator.

**Will you write "massage" on the receipt for a Vasti session if I ask?**
No. The receipt states the service you received. Vasti sessions are not massage therapy.

**I live in BC or Ontario and see you online. Does the tax credit apply?**
The CRA list for BC and Ontario includes registered massage therapists, but an online consultation is not massage, and Ayurvedic practitioners are not on any province's list. The tax credit would not apply to online consultations.

**Can Ayurvedachalam send the claim to my insurer for me?**
That is being set up. Until the pricing page says otherwise, you pay at the time of your visit and claim the receipt yourself.
