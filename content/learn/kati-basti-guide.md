# What Is Kati Basti (Kati Vasti)?

*By Maheswari Vedhachalam, BAMS (Bachelor of Ayurveda Medicine and Surgery) · Ayurveda Practitioner, Calgary · Reviewed [date set in CMS]*

Kati Basti is an Ayurvedic therapy in which warm herbal oil is pooled on the lower back and held there for twenty to thirty minutes inside a ring of dough. It is one of the most asked-about therapies among people with a stiff, tired or aching lower back, and one of the least understood, partly because of its name. This article explains what Kati Basti is and is not, what a session involves, what the evidence looks like, how many sessions people usually book, and who should avoid it.

## First, the name: Basti, Vasti, and what this is not

You will see the therapy written as Kati Basti and Kati Vasti; they are the same word in two transliterations. Kati means the lower back and hips. Vasti means a container, something that holds. In Kati Vasti the container is a ring of black-gram or wheat-flour dough pressed onto the skin, and what it holds is warm oil.

The same word, Vasti, is also used in classical Ayurveda for internal enema therapies. Those are a different thing entirely. This practice does not offer internal Vasti of any kind; in Alberta, procedures of that sort are restricted to regulated health professionals. Everything on this page, and on this site, is the external oil-pooling therapy. Its cousins are Janu Vasti (the knees) and Greeva Vasti (the neck), which work the same way on a different joint.

## What happens in a 45-minute session

1. **Check-in (5 minutes).** Where the discomfort sits, what makes it better or worse, how you slept. On a first visit this follows a consultation.
2. **Positioning.** You lie face down, clothed from the waist down with the lower back exposed, supported with a bolster under the ankles so the spine is comfortable.
3. **The dough ring.** A ring about the size of a saucer is shaped from fresh dough and pressed onto the lower back around the area of discomfort, sealed so it will hold oil.
4. **The oil (25 to 30 minutes).** Warm herbal oil, chosen for you and usually sesame-based, is poured into the ring until it covers the skin. As it cools it is drawn off with cotton and replaced with warm oil, so the area stays at a steady, comfortable warmth throughout.
5. **Gentle work and rest (10 minutes).** The ring and oil are removed, the oil is worked lightly into the surrounding muscles, and you rest for a few minutes before getting up slowly. Herbal steam to the area can be added as a $25 extra.

After the session the skin stays oiled; keep the back warm and covered on the way home, which in a Calgary winter matters, and avoid a cold shower or a cold draught for the rest of the day.

## What do people come to Kati Basti for?

In Ayurvedic terms Kati Vasti is a warming, nourishing therapy for an area where Vata, the quality of dryness and movement, has settled: stiffness that is worst in the morning or after sitting, a back that tires rather than one that is sharply injured, and the general wear of desk work, driving and winter. That is the tradition's description, and in practice it matches who books it.

Clients commonly report that the back feels warm and loose immediately afterwards, that the stiffness on getting out of bed is less for a day or two after a session, and that the effect lasts longer with each session in a series. Many combine it with Abhyanga or with the stretches and routine we set out in a consultation.

## What does the research say?

Very little that would satisfy a sceptic, and it is better to say so. The published literature on Kati Basti consists of small studies and case reports, mostly from Indian Ayurvedic colleges, and almost always with Kati Basti given alongside herbs, diet and other therapies, so the effect of the oil-pooling alone cannot be separated out. A 2025 case report in an integrative-medicine journal describes a 66-year-old woman with two years of chronic low back pain whose disability score fell from severe to minimal over an eight-week programme that included weekly Kati Basti, herbs, dietary changes and yoga; the authors themselves note that a single case with several interventions cannot show which part helped, and call for controlled trials.

So what I can say is this: warmth applied to a sore back for half an hour is a sensible, low-risk thing that people have done in every culture, Kati Vasti is a particularly thorough and pleasant way to do it, and clients value it. What I cannot say is that it treats or cures back conditions, and I will not. Persistent back pain, pain with numbness or weakness in a leg, pain after a fall, or pain with fever or unexplained loss of weight needs your physician first.

## How many sessions should you book?

- **One session** tells you whether you like it. Almost everyone does.
- **Three to five sessions** over two to three weeks is the usual course in the tradition and the pattern my clients describe as making a difference to morning stiffness. The five-session package exists for this.
- **Maintenance** once a month through the winter is common among people who sit for work.

A consultation first is worth it if your back has been troubling you for more than a few weeks, because the routine, sleeping position and a few stretches often do as much as the therapy.

## Who should avoid Kati Basti

Kati Vasti is generally well tolerated. It is not offered, or is postponed, when:

- you are pregnant;
- you have a fever, an acute infection, or an acute injury to the back in the last 72 hours;
- you have an open wound, rash, burn or skin infection on the lower back;
- you have had spinal surgery within the last six months, or have a spinal implant, without your surgeon's agreement;
- you have numbness, tingling or weakness in a leg, or any loss of bladder or bowel control, which needs a physician the same day, not a therapy;
- you have reduced skin sensation (for example from diabetes or a nerve condition), because you may not feel if the oil is too warm; tell us and we will adjust;
- you have a known sesame or nut allergy (another base oil can be used; tell us when booking).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Kati Basti painful?**
No. It is warm oil resting on the skin. The only sensation most people describe is heat spreading through the back and, by the end, drowsiness.

**Kati Basti or a massage: which should I book for a sore back?**
If the back is tired and stiff rather than injured, Kati Vasti is the more targeted choice. If the whole body is tense, Abhyanga. Many clients do both in one visit. A free call will sort it out in five minutes.

**Can I claim Kati Basti on my health benefits?**
Not as massage therapy. Kati Vasti is not a massage, so it is not receipted as one even though our practitioner is a Registered Massage Therapist. If it is combined with Abhyanga, only the massage portion is receipted.

**What oil is used?**
A sesame-based herbal oil in most cases, warmed to a comfortable temperature and tested on your wrist first. The choice of oil is made at your consultation and noted in your file.
